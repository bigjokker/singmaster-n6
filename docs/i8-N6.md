# No extra left-half column for \(C(F_{18}F_{19},F_{16}F_{19})\)

This note records a theorem about one binomial value. It does **not**
prove Singmaster’s conjecture.

## 1. Statement

Let \(F_n\) be the Fibonacci sequence with \(F_1=1\), \(F_2=1\). Write

\[
N=F_{18}F_{19}=10803704,\qquad
K=F_{16}F_{19}=4126647,\qquad
m=C(N,K).
\]

(The complementary family column is \(K+1=F_{17}F_{18}=4126648\). Never
confuse \(K\) with \(F_{18}F_{17}\).) Then \(m\) has
\(\lfloor\log_{10}m\rfloor+1=3120256\) decimal digits, and

\[
\log_{10}m=3120255.2212,\qquad
k_{\max}=5182637,
\]

where \(k_{\max}\) is the largest integer with \(C(2k,k)\le m\).

Following the engine’s count, the multiplicity is

\[
N(m)=2+\sum 2
\]

with two trivials \(C(m,1)=C(m,m-1)=m\), plus two appearances for each
off-centre left-half pair \(C(n,k)=C(n,n-k)=m\) with \(2\le k<n/2\).
The Lind–Singmaster–Tovey identity supplies exactly two such pairs,

\[
C(N,K)=C(N-1,K+1)=m,
\]

hence \(N(m)\ge 6\). There is no central solution \(C(2k,k)=m\).

**Theorem.** There is no pair \((n,k)\) with \(2\le k\le n/2\),
\(k\notin\{K,K+1\}\), and \(C(n,k)=m\). Therefore \(N(m)=6\).

## 2. The image of a column

**Lemma (complete image).** Let \(p\) be prime and \(k<p\). Then

\[
\{C(n,k)\bmod p:n\ge k\}
=\{0\}\cup\bigl\{C(k+j,j)\bmod p:0\le j<p-k\bigr\}.
\]

Write \(I_{p,k}\) for the right-hand side. Equivalently, with \(g=p-k\),

\[
C(k+j,j)\equiv(-1)^j C(g-1,j)\pmod p\qquad(0\le j<g).
\]

(The identity uses \(C(k+j,j)\), not \(C(k,j)\).)

*Proof sketch.* Lucas, one digit of \(k\): \(k<p\), so
\(C(n,k)\equiv C(n_0,k)\pmod p\) with \(n_0=n\bmod p\). As \(n_0\) runs
through \(\{k,\ldots,p-1\}\) one gets \(C(k+j,k)=C(k+j,j)\) for
\(0\le j<g\), together with \(0\) when \(n_0<k\). For \(n\ge p\) the
same residues repeat. \(\square\)

**Corollary.** If \(r(p):=m\bmod p\) does not lie in \(I_{p,k}\), then
no \(n\) satisfies \(C(n,k)=m\). The obstruction is a certificate for
the whole column \(k\), not a sample.

Left-half solutions require \(k\le n/2\), hence \(n\ge 2k\) and
\(C(n,k)\ge C(2k,k)\). For \(k>k_{\max}\) one has \(C(2k,k)>m\), so no
left-half solution exists. Extra left-half columns can only occur for

\[
2\le k\le k_{\max},\qquad k\notin\{K,K+1\}.
\]

(The two trivials are \(k=1\), never in this range.)

## 3. Where a prime can kill

Two-digit primes are \(3287\le p\le K\). Write
\(\alpha=\lfloor N/p\rfloor\), \(\beta=\lfloor K/p\rfloor\),
\(d=N-K=6677057\).

**Digit-0.** \(m\equiv 0\pmod p\) iff \(p(\alpha-\beta)>d\). Then
\(0\in I_{p,k}\) and \(p\) cannot kill.

**Live two-digit primes.** If \(p(\alpha-\beta)\le d\) then
\(m\equiv C(\alpha,\beta)C(n_0,k_0)\not\equiv 0\pmod p\) with
\(n_0=N-\alpha p\), \(k_0=K-\beta p\). Survivors are image matches
only. The same holds on the PART-lower side of a mixed cell
(\(p<z_{\mathrm{lo}}\)).

**Z-width.** The cell of \((\alpha,\beta)\) is
\(P_{\mathrm{lo}}=\max(\lfloor N/(\alpha+1)\rfloor+1,\lfloor K/(\beta+1)\rfloor+1)\),
\(P_{\mathrm{hi}}=\min(\lfloor N/\alpha\rfloor,\lfloor K/\beta\rfloor)\),
\(z_{\mathrm{lo}}=\lfloor d/(\alpha-\beta)\rfloor+1\). FULL / PART / NONE
according to where \(z_{\mathrm{lo}}\) sits. A Z-slab can be skipped:
the first live prime after \(k\) is the first NONE or PART-lower prime
above the slab, or else a prime \(>N/2\).

**Band II zero block.** For \(K<p\le N/2\) one has \(\alpha\ge 2\) and
\(p\mid m\), so those primes cannot kill. The first possible Band II
kill is the first prime \(>N/2=5401852\).

**Band II live window.** On \(N/2<p\le d\) one has \(\alpha=1\),
\(\beta=0\), and \(r(p)=C(N-p,K)\not\equiv 0\). With \(\delta=2p-N\),

\[
r(p)\equiv(-1)^K C(K+\delta-1,\delta-1)\pmod p.
\]

At \(p_1=5401853\), \(\delta=2\) and \(K\) is odd, so
\(r(p_1)=-(K+1)=1275205\). (Factorial table, falling product, closed
form, and Lucas all agree.)

## 4. Size of the image

\(I_g\) is the coefficient set of \((1-X)^{g-1}\) over \(\mathbf{F}_p\),
\(g<p\). No coefficient vanishes. The only systematic collision is
Pascal reflection \(j\leftrightarrow g-1-j\):

- \(g\) odd: fold, \(\lvert I_g\rvert\approx(g+1)/2\);
- \(g\) even: \(v\mapsto -v\), no fold, \(\lvert I_g\rvert\approx g\).

The rest is the birthday count \(p\bigl(1-(1-1/p)^M\bigr)\) with
\(M=g\) or \((g+1)/2\), matching computed rows to \(0.1\%\). Prime gaps
are even, so the parity of \(g=p-k\) is constant along a chain of
consecutive live primes: even-\(g\) columns stay about twice as easy.

Independence after this size law predicted the fat-image triples, the
walk-369 cascade \(44/6/0.8/0.1\), the straggler \(3.97/4\), and the
Band II kill curve. A character-sum bound on
\(\mathbf{1}_{I_{g_1}}(r(p_1))\mathbf{1}_{I_{g_2}}(r(p_2))\) is the
wrong shape (completion loses the fold; Weil is vacuous for
\(j>\sqrt{p}\)).

The computational kernel is the equivalent factorial test: with
\(F[x]=x!\bmod p\),

\[
k\text{ survives }p
\iff
\exists\,b\in[0,p-k-1]\ \ F[k+b]\equiv r(p)\,F[k]\,F[b]\pmod p.
\]

Here \(b\) is the same index \(j\) as in \(I_g\). int64 is safe:
\(p^2<2^{63}\) throughout the range used.

## 5. Exhaustion

Every extra \(k\) in \(\{2,\ldots,k_{\max}\}\setminus\{K,K+1\}\) was
given a live prime with \(r(p)\notin I_{p,k}\).

## 6. What this is not

- Not Singmaster’s conjecture (a bound on \(N(t)\) for every \(t\)).
- Not a statement about \(i=9,10\) or any other family member.
- Not a licence to next-prime Band II from \(k\), to build \(m_{10}\),
  or to treat nearby sampled nulls as certificates.

The method — complete column image, Z-jump, Band II window, factorial
kernel — does not use the decimal expansion of \(m\) and applies to any
fixed pair \((N,K)\).
